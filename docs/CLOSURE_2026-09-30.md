# Closure of this line of work, 30 September 2026

This file records that I stopped pursuing publication of the work in this
archive, what further checks I ran before deciding, which claims those checks
withdrew, and which one survived. It is written for someone who downloads this
deposit and needs to know how far to trust each part of it.

Nothing in the archive has been deleted. The superseded analyses, the figures
built for the withdrawn manuscript, and the result files that contradict it are
all left in place. This notice is what marks them.

## 2. What I ran after v1.3.0, and why

Two alternative explanations for the central result had not been excluded, and
both are the kind that an archive should answer rather than leave to a reader.
`code/84_depth_and_composition.py` states the decision rule in its own docstring,
above the code, before any of the numbers below were computed.

```
D1  If under B the module's mean shift loses its sign, or its percentile rises
    above the 25th, in the majority of the proteomes where B can be run, the
    module claim is driven by differential dropout and the work is closed.
D2  If under C the implied renormalization shift accounts for more than half
    of the plasma class's observed rise in a cohort, no claim about plasma
    proteins rising may be made from that cohort.
D3  The module claim survives only if it passes D1 and its fall is larger than
    the implied compositional shift by at least a factor of three.
```

**Test A, detection asymmetry.** Tumor arms are measured more deeply than
adjacent arms in three of the four proteomes, by 17.0%, 14.6% and 26.5% more
identifications per sample. A protein that is harder to detect in the shallower
arm can appear to fall without falling.

**Test B, complete-case restriction.** Every contrast recomputed on only those
proteins quantified in both arms of every pair, which removes the asymmetry by
construction.

| Cohort | Depth asymmetry | Complete-case proteins | Module members | Module mean *z* | Surviving share | Percentile among non-secreted |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Jiang 2019 | +17.0% | 1,229 (13.5%) | 2 | -0.900 | 1.000 | 6.24 |
| Yi 2023 | +0.0% | 3,678 (75.3%) | 5 | -0.715 | 0.954 | 8.82 |
| MSKCC-ICC | +14.6% | 443 (5.2%) | 2 | -1.858 | 1.000 | 2.73 |
| UKF-ICC | +26.5% | 1,350 (17.9%) | 1 | -1.858 | 1.000 | 0.33 |

D1 passes. The module keeps its sign and stays below the 10th percentile in all
four, including Yi 2023, where the depth asymmetry is zero and three quarters of
the matrix is complete.

**Test C, compositional accounting.** If the module's proteins carry a
measurable share of total signal, their loss renormalizes everything else upward
even when nothing else changes. The implied lift is
`-log2(1 - mass_lost)`, computed from the module's share of linear signal in
each arm, and compared with the raw paired log2 differences so that the units
match.

| Cohort | Implied lift (log2) | Plasma raw log2 | Share of plasma rise explained by composition | Module raw log2 | Module / lift |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Jiang 2019 | +0.0116 | +0.536 | 0.022 | -1.880 | 162.5x |
| Yi 2023 | +0.0479 | +0.184 | 0.260 | -1.234 | 25.8x |
| MSKCC-ICC | +0.0257 | +0.280 | 0.092 | -5.899 | 229.8x |
| UKF-ICC | +0.0260 | +0.061 | 0.425 | -4.487 | 172.7x |

D2 passes, since composition never accounts for more than half of the plasma
class's rise. D3 passes by 25.8x to 229.8x against a required factor of three.

**Class means against the background of the same matrix**, complete cases, in
*z* units. This comparison was not in the earlier analyses and it is the one
that changed my mind about the plasma class.

| Cohort | Background | Module | Module - background | Plasma | Plasma - background |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Jiang 2019 | +0.321 | -0.900 | -1.221 | +0.559 | +0.238 |
| Yi 2023 | +0.205 | -0.715 | -0.920 | +0.142 | -0.063 |
| MSKCC-ICC | -0.042 | -1.858 | -1.816 | +0.656 | +0.697 |
| UKF-ICC | +0.550 | -1.858 | -2.408 | +0.053 | -0.497 |

**Test D, cross-level concordance within one cohort.** `code/83_gao_within_sample.py`
uses the per-gene transcript-to-protein Spearman correlations that Gao et al.
published for the same 159 pairs. The metabolic class has a median rho of +0.853,
at the 96.8th percentile of 6,203 genes, against +0.319 at the 19.1th percentile
for the plasma class, Mann-Whitney *P* = 0.0005. *ARG1* is at +0.890, *OTC* at
+0.886 and *CPS1* at +0.886. *ALB* is at -0.072, the 0.4th percentile.

Two limits on that test, both stated before it was run. The correlations are
computed across pooled tumor and adjacent samples, so they are not independent of
the effect they help explain; and the prediction that the plasma class would show
*negative* concordance failed, since it is weakly positive. The class difference
is in how tightly the two levels track, not in opposite directions.

## 3. Claims withdrawn

**The albumin and plasma-protein claim is withdrawn.** The drafted manuscript led
with a warning that albumin is not a usable hepatocyte identity marker in a tumor
proteome, resting on the plasma class rising while the metabolic class falls. The
background-corrected table above does not support a claim about the class: it sits
above the background of its own matrix in two cohorts and below it in two. What
survives is the single-gene observation about *ALB* in Test D, in one cohort, from
someone else's published correlation table, subject to the pooled-arm limit.

**"The sign of a near-zero composite is arbitrary" is withdrawn as stated.** The
section dated 20 September in `README.md` says the 22-gene composite averages to
near zero and that its sign is therefore arbitrary. Recomputed from the raw
matrices, three of the four proteome composites are statistically significant:
Gao -0.139 at *P* = 1.65e-04, Jiang +0.089 at *P* = 0.0318, and the Yi DIA-NN
matrix +0.384 at *P* = 0.0026, against Yi EncyclopeDIA -0.149 at *P* = 0.106. The
defensible statement is narrower and is the one to use: the composite's **sign is
unstable across cohorts and across quantification pipelines applied to the same
raw files**, while the class split underneath it is stable. A significance test on
the composite is not uninformative; it is answering a question whose answer
changes with the cohort.

**The held-out result holds in the primary matrix only, and the earlier wording
did not say so.** `README.md` states that all the predictions for Yi 2023 held.
That is correct for EncyclopeDIA, the primary matrix, chosen on completeness
before any contrast was computed. It is not correct for DIA-NN, one of the two
sensitivity matrices built from the same raw files, where H1 and H3 both fail
(`results/HOLDOUT_Yi2023_DIANN.json`, `passes: false` in each). The third matrix,
Spectronaut, fails the sample identity gate and is reported as not testable. A
reader should treat the held-out confirmation as resting on one of three
quantifications of one cohort.

**Two registered predictions failed in the cholangiocarcinoma cohorts, not one.**
`README.md` describes G3, the prediction that plasma proteins would fall. G4, the
prediction that the gap between the plasma and metabolic classes would be smaller
than in the hepatocellular proteomes, also failed: the gap is 2.318 and 1.895
against 1.827, 1.413 and 0.857, so it is larger, not smaller
(`results/ICCA_VALIDATION.json`, `smaller_than_all_hcc: false`). G4 was registered
as directional only, so its failure retracts nothing, but it belongs in the
summary alongside G3.

**A class that was never reported.** The five transcription factors reverse
between measurement levels more strongly than the ten plasma proteins do, at
+0.788 and +0.925 against +0.408 and +0.514, with surviving shares of 0.511 and
0.189 in the transcriptomes. Any reading of the reversal that appeals to secretion
has to account for a nuclear class that reverses harder than the secreted one.
`results/PANEL_COHERENCE.json` carries these numbers; no document in earlier
versions drew attention to them.

## 4. What survives

One claim, and it is narrower than the archive's earlier framing.

> In paired liver tumor and adjacent tissue, a module of urea cycle and
> gluconeogenic enzymes (*CPS1*, *OTC*, *ARG1*, *PCK1*) falls further than almost
> anything else in the measured proteome, consistently across four independent
> proteomes and two tumor types; the fall survives restriction to completely
> detected proteins and is one to two orders of magnitude larger than any
> compositional artifact could produce; and in the one cohort where matched
> transcript and protein measurements exist, these enzymes are among the most
> tightly concordant genes in the proteome while albumin is among the least.

I did not pursue publication of this claim, for a reason that is about the
literature rather than about the evidence. Down-regulation of the urea cycle in
hepatocellular carcinoma has already been published as a proteomic signature:
"Significant Down-Regulation of Urea Cycle Generates Clinically Relevant
Proteomic Signature in Hepatocellular Carcinoma," *J Proteome Res*
2019;18(5):2032, doi 10.1021/acs.jproteome.8b00921. I cite it by title because I
reached the publisher's landing page and the abstract but not the full author
list, and I do not state an author list I have not read. What this archive adds
to that is quantitative extremity and
cross-level concordance, which is a methodological refinement rather than a new
finding, and four desk rejections without reviewer contact were evidence enough
about how such a refinement reads to editors.

## 5. What the archive is still good for

The deposit stands on its own terms as a record of method rather than as support
for a published article.

* 764 paired samples across eight datasets, with every matrix's premises checked
  before any contrast was computed, and two matrices reported as not testable
  rather than analyzed.
* Predictions, thresholds and decision rules written as code constants before the
  data were obtained, in `code/77_holdout_validation.py` and
  `code/84_depth_and_composition.py`, with the one part written afterward marked
  as such in its own docstring.
* Every failed prediction reported: H-series in
  `results/HOLDOUT_Yi2023_*.json`, G-series in `results/ICCA_VALIDATION.json`,
  the secretome hypothesis in `results/SECRETOME_TRANSFER.json` and
  `results/SECRETOME_VERIFY.json`, and the plasma concordance prediction in
  `results/GAO_WITHIN_SAMPLE.json`.
* Two corrections to my own statistics, with the superseded values kept rather
  than overwritten, in `docs/PROTOCOL_DEVIATIONS.md`.
* One protocol deviation, recorded with what was known at the time and with the
  affected results demoted to exploratory.

Anyone reusing the module should read section 3 first and treat section 4 as the
whole of what the data support.

## 6. Housekeeping corrected in v1.3.1

Three things in the deposit were stale or inconsistent rather than wrong in
substance, and are fixed here.

* `README.md` described the article as "under review". Four venues had declined
  it by 29 September 2026. The line now states the publication status.
* `.zenodo.json` supplies the description shown on the Zenodo record, and it
  still led with the 20 September correction, including the sentence about the
  composite's sign that section 3 withdraws, and did not mention the held-out
  cohorts added in v1.3.0. It is rewritten to lead with this closure notice.
* `retentionfrac` declared two different versions. `tool/pyproject.toml` said
  1.0.0 while `tool/retentionfrac/__init__.py` said 1.0.1, so an installed copy
  reported one number at runtime and another in its package metadata. The
  archive does not record which was intended and there is no changelog for the
  package, so I made the metadata agree with the module's own value, 1.0.1,
  because that is what a user querying the installed package sees. No code in
  the package changed in this version.

One thing I checked and did not change. A scratch file named
`results/HOLDOUT_VALIDATION.json` in my working tree is byte-identical to the
DIA-NN sensitivity run and was flagged during the audit as a possible deposit
defect. It is not in the deposit and never was. The deposit carries the three
runs under their own names, `results/HOLDOUT_Yi2023_EncyclopeDIA.json`,
`results/HOLDOUT_Yi2023_DIANN.json` and `results/HOLDOUT_Yi2023_Spectronaut.json`,
each labeled with which quantification it came from and whether each prediction
passed. I record the check here so that the flag does not resurface as an
unresolved item.

## 7. A deposit incident, found and corrected on 30 September 2026

Two Zenodo versions of this archive carry a version label that does not match
their files. I record it here because a reader who cites one of them needs to
know, and because the archive's whole claim on a reader's trust is that it says
what went wrong.

### What happened

Two releases were prepared, v1.3.0 and v1.3.1, and for each one a Zenodo version
DOI was minted. Neither release's content ever reached the GitHub repository.
The commits were built and tagged in a local clone and the push did not complete,
while the GitHub release was created through the web interface, which creates the
tag at the current head of the default branch. That head was still the v1.2.4
commit. Zenodo archived what the tag pointed at.

Checked directly against the remote on 30 September 2026, four references all
resolved to the same commit, `8d68685`, which is v1.2.4:

| Reference | Commit |
|---|---|
| `refs/heads/main` | `8d68685` |
| `refs/tags/v1.2.4` | `8d68685` |
| `refs/tags/v1.3.0` | `8d68685` |
| `refs/tags/v1.3.1` | `8d68685` |

### Which deposits are affected

| Version DOI | Labeled | Files actually deposited |
|---|---|---|
| 10.5281/zenodo.23052241 | v1.3.0 | the v1.2.4 tree, 259 files |
| 10.5281/zenodo.23054376 | v1.3.1 | the v1.2.4 tree, 259 files |
| 10.5281/zenodo.22759386 | v1.2.0 | empty, from an earlier build error, recorded at the time |

Zenodo versions cannot be withdrawn, so these records remain. Their metadata has
been annotated to say what they contain.

**Anything a reader wants from v1.3.0 or v1.3.1 is in v1.3.2 and not in those two
deposits.** That includes `docs/PREREGISTRATION_HELD_OUT_COHORTS.md`,
`docs/PROTOCOL_DEVIATIONS.md`, `code/77_holdout_validation.py`,
`code/78_icca_validation.py`, `results/ICCA_VALIDATION.json`, the three
`results/HOLDOUT_Yi2023_*.json` files, and this closure notice with
`code/83_gao_within_sample.py` and `code/84_depth_and_composition.py`.

What the affected deposits do contain, correctly, is the v1.2.4 tree, including
`docs/PREREGISTRATION.md`, `docs/PREREGISTRATION_with_amendments.md` and a
`SHA256SUMS.txt` that verifies those 259 files. The concept DOI
10.5281/zenodo.22658669 is unaffected and resolves to the latest version.

### Why it was not caught

I confirmed that each DOI existed and recorded the release as complete without
checking what the DOI resolved to. That is the same mistake this archive
documents elsewhere: establishing that a file or record exists and treating that
as evidence about its contents. A release is not verified by the identifier
coming back; it is verified by reading the deposited tree.

### What changed so that it cannot repeat silently

The release procedure now reads the remote back after pushing and fails if the
remote reference does not resolve to the commit just created, and it pushes the
branch before creating the tag. The release on the hosting service must be
created from a tag that already exists on the remote, never by asking the
interface to create a new tag, because that places the tag on the default
branch's head rather than on the intended commit. That single step is what
produced this incident.
