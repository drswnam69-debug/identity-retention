# Supplementary Material 3. Supplementary Note: analyses reported in full

**Nam SW.** *How much of a tumor expression signature survives adjustment for the dominant tumor-adjacent axis? A pre-registered measure in three tissues and its failure to transfer between a transcriptome and a proteome.*

This note carries, in full and unaltered, twelve analyses that the main text now reports in summary. Nothing here was removed from the record; it was moved so that the main text carries one argument rather than every analysis that argument rests on. Each section states the amendment that governs it, and every number in this note is bound to the same result archive, and checked by the same script (`50_consistency_check.py` with `52_anchored_claims.py`), as every number in the main text.

The registered verdicts stated here are the verdicts of record. Where a sensitivity was run after a pre-submission review rather than under the plan, the text says so in the same sentence.

## Notation, and how to read this note

This note is written to be read beside the main article, and every cross-reference to a Figure or a Table in it, and every bracketed citation, points into that article rather than into this file. The covariates and modules it uses are defined in the article's Methods and are repeated here so that the note can be followed on its own.

**D1** is the pre-specified 22-gene hepatocyte-identity score; **D2** is an over-adjusted variant that adds four cytochrome P450 genes. **C1** is a disjoint 21-gene non-parenchymal content score; **C2** adds four biliary and epithelial markers. **K1** and **L1** are the corresponding identity scores for kidney and for lung, each written out before that tissue's matrix was opened. **REDUCTION**, **DRAIN** and **SUPPLY** are the three modules of the worked example, and **DRAIN′** is DRAIN with *POR* removed under §6l. The **identity-retention fraction** is the intercept of a signature's paired-difference regression on the covariates, as a proportion of its unadjusted paired shift.

Abbreviations used here: CI, confidence interval; HCC, hepatocellular carcinoma; HR, hazard ratio; IQR, interquartile range; MASLD, metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease; NMD, nonsense-mediated decay; TCGA, The Cancer Genome Atlas; VIF, variance inflation factor.

The five works cited by number in this note are, in the article's numbering: [3] Grinchuk et al., *Mol Oncol* 2018; [4] Pinyol et al., *J Hepatol* 2021; [21] Gao et al., *Cell* 2019; [22] Jiang et al., *Nature* 2019; [31] Liberzon et al., *Bioinformatics* 2011.

## S1. Composition adjustment removes almost none of the composite effect in liver

The non-parenchymal score C1 was lower in tumor in all three cohorts (*P* = 8.9 × 10⁻⁵, 4.1 × 10⁻⁴ and 0.005), which is what a diseased comparator predicts. The comparator is diseased in each: cirrhosis was recorded in **54%** of the 115 patients from whom GSE76427 was assembled and in **92%** of the 206 GSE14520 patients, both figures from the clinicopathological table of the study that assembled the two cohorts together [3], and GSE164760's non-tumor samples are livers with steatohepatitis [4]. Adjusting for C1 alone did not reduce the composite effect at all (101% and 116% retained). The joint model is a different matter in the smaller cohort, and §6j asks for that verdict in words: in GSE76427 it leaves 0.399 of 1.129, so there the composite rise is partly explained by the two covariates together. That cohort's D1-alone fraction, 0.5031, sits three thousandths above the §6g threshold and a rebuild from the deposited matrix puts it at 0.4619, on the other side; a verdict decided by three thousandths and reversed by a rebuild is not a finding, and I report it as undetermined rather than as a pass. The covariates were near-independent (variance inflation 1.12 and 1.14), and the pre-specified C2 markers changed nothing material (43% and 64%; Table 2).

## S2. Robustness of the worked example

Four post hoc checks qualify the composite verdicts (§6q), three of which were computed before the amendment recording them was written. The 50% rule was applied to point estimates; bootstrapping the ratio gives intervals 0.301 to 0.759, 0.095 to 0.706, 0.450 to 0.704 and 0.452 to 0.736, none of which excludes 0.50, so for the composite the registered verdicts stand as rules but the data do not separate the two sides of the threshold. ΔD1 = 0 is sparsely observed (6 of 52 and 11 of 213 pairs at ΔD1 ≥ 0), making the intercept an extrapolation. A quadratic term is unnecessary in GSE76427 (*P* = 0.22) but warranted in GSE14520 (*P* = 6.0 × 10⁻⁴), where it moves composite retention from 63% to 58%; §6ae extends that test from the composite to all 119 signatures. Heteroscedasticity-consistent standard errors of the HC3 form widen the standard error on the composite intercept without moving it across zero. In GSE14520 the increase is **50%**, 0.068 to 0.102, and recomputation from the archive reproduces it. In GSE76427 §6q recorded 17%, and recomputation gives 29% instead, 0.140 to 0.179; this is the same cohort whose covariate cannot be rebuilt from the deposited matrix, so I report both rather than choose between them. The amendment also took its HC3 intervals from a normal critical value, the shortcut §6n corrected elsewhere in this pipeline; recomputed with the t critical value the interval is 0.591 to 0.992 in GSE14520 and 0.161 to 0.882 in GSE76427, and neither contains zero. These qualifications apply to the composite; the module gap in GSE14520 is 89% against 31%, and the §6v bootstrap intervals on those two fractions, 0.738 to 1.036 and 0.033 to 0.492, do not approach each other.

Because *POR* entered DRAIN on a mistaken premise (§6l), every analysis in the two paired cohorts that §6l named was recomputed with DRAIN′ = mean *z* (*MTARC1*, *MTARC2*) under a rule fixed beforehand. The GSE14520 separation was intact, REDUCTION retaining 89.0% against DRAIN′ at 31.8% versus 31.0% with *POR*, and GSE76427 again failing to separate the arms. A reader who prefers the two-gene arm can read the conclusions of these two cohorts off that analysis. That equivalence does not hold in TCGA-LIHC, where the two versions differ qualitatively, as reported below.

## S3. Retention does not predict whether a signature's prognostic value is independent of the identity score

A measure that describes what a contrast is made of is worth more if it says something a reader would act on. There is one obvious place to look. Tumor differentiation grade is recorded in routine pathology, and a signature that predicts outcome only through it would be restating something the pathology report already contains. Under §6z, written before any survival value was read, I tested whether the identity-retention fraction detects that (Supplementary Table S7).

In TCGA-LIHC, 365 primary tumors carry overall survival with **131 deaths**, which is the first adequately powered survival analysis in this study; the earlier one had 23 events. The identity score itself is only weakly prognostic here, with a hazard ratio of 0.886 per standard deviation (0.781 to 1.005, *P* = 0.061) and no violation of proportional hazards (Schoenfeld *P* = 0.92), so there was less for the test to detect than the premise of the question assumes.

Of the 103 evaluable signatures, **82 were prognostic** before adjustment at *P* < 0.05 and 78 survived Benjamini-Hochberg control at 5%. After adjusting each for the tumor's own identity score, only **9 of the 82** lost significance. The pre-registered primary test, the correlation between retention and the fraction of the log hazard ratio surviving adjustment, gave **Spearman ρ = +0.296 (*P* = 0.0024)**. The direction is the expected one and the *P* value is small, but §6z fixed the threshold at ρ ≥ 0.30, and 0.296 does not meet it. **The registered verdict is therefore that the association does not reach the size fixed for it, so retention is not established as a marker of prognostic independence and no clinical claim is made anywhere in this paper.** I report the miss rather than round it, because a threshold that is only binding when it is convenient is not a threshold.

The registered estimate is also weaker than it looks, and in the direction that supports its own verdict. Twenty-one of the 103 signatures are not prognostic before adjustment at all, so for them the surviving fraction is a ratio of two quantities indistinguishable from zero, which is exactly the instability the §6v guard exists to catch elsewhere in this paper. Restricted to the **82 that are prognostic** at *P* < 0.05 the correlation falls to **ρ = +0.213 (*P* = 0.055)**, and restricted to the 78 that survive Benjamini-Hochberg control it falls to **ρ = +0.106 (*P* = 0.36)**. This sensitivity was not registered and I did not run it until a pre-submission review asked for it, so the all-signature estimate remains the estimate of record. What it changes is a sentence I would otherwise have written: the association is not separable from zero once signatures with no prognostic signal to lose are set aside, so calling it present but too small understates how little is there.

A descriptive pattern in the registered direction is nonetheless present and is reported as descriptive only: splitting at the median retention, none of the 42 prognostic high-retention signatures lost significance after adjustment, against 9 of the 40 low-retention ones. That is not the pre-registered test and does not license the claim the pre-registered test failed to support.

The substantive result here is the one that was not the question. **The prognostic value of published liver signatures is, in this cohort, largely not attributable to the tumor's own hepatocyte-identity score**: 73 of 82 kept their signal after adjustment, and that score was itself only weakly prognostic (*P* = 0.061). Because §6ae shows D1 is not distinguished from a covariate of its size, I state this about the score and not about differentiation state. That is consistent with the benchmark section, where three quarters of signatures retained at least half of their tumor-adjacent contrast, and it argues against the suspicion that motivated this measure rather than for it.

## S4. Which liver cells carry the panel genes

Under the 50% threshold fixed in §6m, the premise that the panel genes are hepatocyte genes holds for two of the three DRAIN genes and for none of the four REDUCTION genes (Table 4), *NQO1* being lowest at 0.006 and *MTARC1* falling short at 0.385. All three DRAIN genes also stain more strongly in hepatocytes than cholangiocytes. The same source contradicts its own single-cell data for *CYB5R3* and *CYB5R1*, which stain at medium intensity in hepatocytes against single-cell hepatocyte fractions of 0.041 and 0.131, and for *MTARC1*, which stains medium against 1.0 normalized counts per million; a recovery bias against hepatocytes in droplet-based protocols would predict exactly this, although I did not test it and cite no source for it. One coincidence is worth stating and not building on: the arm whose hepatocyte origin is best supported is also the arm whose transcript shift D1 absorbs most, and under §6ae that second fact is not evidence about identity.

## S5. The composite index, its supply arm, and the pre-specified secondary outcomes

SUPPLY contributes 15 of the index's 22 genes and half its positive weight and does not behave like the other modules (§6r). Across the MASLD gradient it was the weakest arm (*z* = 1.35, *P* = 0.089). Between tumor and adjacent liver it was absent in GSE76427 (−0.036, *P* = 0.57) but strongly present in GSE14520 (+0.336, *P* = 6.8 × 10⁻¹⁶). In GSE14520 it retained 109.5% of its shift after joint adjustment, that is, the adjustment removed nothing from it. The reproducible tumor behavior of the composite is carried by REDUCTION and DRAIN, not by the biosynthetic arm, and the composite's name overstates SUPPLY's contribution.

Two further pre-specified sensitivity analyses were performed and are reported here rather than left in the protocol (§6r). Adding the cholesterol-arm genes to SUPPLY left the severity trend essentially unchanged in the discovery cohort, *z* from 5.98 to 5.95 across all 216 samples and 4.82 to 4.76 with the controls excluded, which the protocol fixed in advance as **weakening** rather than supporting the claim that the trend is specific to the coenzyme Q arm. Scoring *CYB5R3* alone in place of REDUCTION gave the same direction more weakly in that cohort (*z* = 4.56 and 3.15). Neither variant was carried to the replication series. Both outcomes are recorded because the protocol required them, not because they help.

**Field effect (H3):** *P* = 0.060, in the predicted direction, which I report without a verdict because the protocol fixed a direction for H3 and no decision rule. Four archived run summaries label it not supported at the conventional 0.05; that label is the run script's default and not a registered rule, so I report the value and the direction and leave the verdict open rather than adopt a threshold the plan did not set. 
**Overall survival (H4):** hazard ratio 0.99 per standard deviation (95% CI 0.62 to 1.56) on 23 events in the univariable model, and 0.95 (0.61 to 1.49) in a model adjusted for age, sex and stage, where Barcelona Clinic Liver Cancer stage C/D carried a hazard ratio of 4.56 (1.44 to 14.4); with 23 events the study has 80% power only for hazard ratios of 1.79 or larger, or 0.56 or smaller. §6d also fixed two secondary analyses that were run and, until this revision, went unreported: the proportional-hazards check on the univariable model is met (Schoenfeld ρ = −0.306, *P* = 0.16), and the pre-specified tertile split of the index (cut-points 0.125 and 0.642; 39, 38 and 38 patients with 8, 7 and 8 deaths) separates nothing, log-rank χ² = 0.37 on 2 degrees of freedom, *P* = 0.83. A third, recurrence-free survival, was fixed as a reported secondary endpoint and was simply skipped; the tidy phenotype table I built carries overall survival only, but the deposited series matrix carries recurrence data for 108 of the patients, so nothing prevented it. Run now, it is the better powered of the two endpoints: 48 events against 23, and the index carries a hazard ratio of **1.31** per standard deviation (0.96 to 1.79, *P* = 0.084), in the predicted direction and short of significance. I report it as the registered secondary it was, and note that the overall-survival result the manuscript leads with rests on a third as many events. 
**Nonsense-mediated decay (NMD) target burden (H5):** §6c fixed this as exploratory whatever the outcome, and its numbers belong here rather than only in the archive. The composite correlated with the NMD target score at ρ = +0.259 (*P* = 1.2 × 10⁻⁴) in the discovery cohort, −0.003 (*P* = 0.98) and +0.150 (*P* = 0.14) in the two replications, and the two pre-specified sensitivity analyses, dropping the ten mitochondrial and the four integrated-stress-response members, moved nothing (+0.242 and +0.264 in the discovery cohort). The result is not interpretable as an NMD signal: the gene-level proxy proved confounded with overall expression (ρ = 0.81 to 0.98), so a transcript-level quantity is not testable this way, and I report the correlations as the exploratory description §6c called for rather than as a test. 
**Ferroptosis directionality (H6):** the suppressor and driver sets are `GOBP_NEGATIVE_REGULATION_OF_FERROPTOSIS` and `GOBP_POSITIVE_REGULATION_OF_FERROPTOSIS` from the Molecular Signatures Database [31]. The FerrDb V2 tables §6d preferred could not be reached, and the two MSigDB sets §6d named as its fallback, `WP_FERROPTOSIS` and `GOBP_FERROPTOSIS`, are not split by direction and so cannot test a directional hypothesis. I substituted the two directional Gene Ontology sets under the clause of §6d that requires the set actually used to be recorded with the result, and I record here that the substitution is mine and was not pre-specified. Four and one of their members respectively are in this study's own panel and were removed before scoring, as §6d required. §6d placed this test in every prepared cohort and it was run in four, of which GSE164760 carried too few of the sets' genes to score, one member of each directional set against the minimum of five, so the result rests on the three severity cohorts, GSE135251, GSE130970 and GSE167523. GSE76427, the cohort §6d made primary for the prognostic hypothesis, was left off the run. That was an omission and not a property of the data: its matrix carries 20,757 symbols. I cannot repair it now, because the membership of the two substituted sets is not in the deposited gene-set files, which is a gap in the deposit rather than in the plan; a later attempt to run it therefore scored nothing, and the archived record of that attempt says in as many words that its zero is the absence of the file and not a property of the cohort. I record both rather than leave the absence unexplained. With every panel gene removed the composite correlated positively with the suppressor set (ρ = +0.439, +0.209, +0.171) *and* the driver set (+0.502, +0.190, +0.217) in all three cohorts. The predicted dissociation did not appear.

## S6. What this does not buy the clinic

The measure was built to describe a contrast, and a fair question is whether describing it changes anything. §6z put that question to the only test this data can support, and the answer was no. Retention missed the registered threshold for predicting whether a signature's prognostic value is independent of the tumor's own identity score, by four thousandths. The interval around it, 0.109 to 0.463, contains the threshold on both sides, and the association is weaker still once the 21 signatures with no prognostic signal are set aside, falling to ρ = +0.213 among the 82 that are prognostic and to +0.106 among the 78 that survive multiplicity control. I report that as the result rather than as a near miss, because the value of a pre-registered threshold is entirely in what happens when it is inconvenient. One thing about it turns on a choice §6z made in advance rather than on the data: the surviving fraction was defined as |log HR adjusted| over |log HR unadjusted|, by deliberate analogy with the retention fraction, and that absolute value folds in 6 of the 103 signatures whose hazard ratio crosses 1.0 under adjustment. On the signed ratio the same correlation is ρ = 0.409, which would have met the threshold. The registered definition is the one I report.

Two things follow. First, no clinical use is claimed for this measure anywhere in this paper. It is a descriptor of what a tumor-adjacent contrast contains, and on this evidence it is not a filter for clinical usefulness. Second, the analysis produced a finding of its own that is more reassuring than the suspicion behind it: in 365 hepatocellular carcinomas, 73 of 82 prognostic signatures kept their prognostic signal after identity adjustment, and identity itself was not significantly prognostic. Taken with the benchmark, where three quarters of signatures retained at least half of their contrast, the picture is of a literature less confounded by the dominant tumor-adjacent axis than I expected when I started.

That the identity score is only weakly prognostic in this cohort is also a limit on the test rather than a finding about differentiation. Pathological grade was not available in the file used, and a 22-gene score is a proxy for it with error. A properly powered version of this question would use grade itself, in more than one cohort, and it is worth doing.

## S7. Behavior of the estimator over the pre-registered simulation grid (§6v)

The estimator recovers a known retention fraction over most of the grid, but not to the standard §6v set for calling it well behaved. Across a pre-registered grid of 34 conditions, with synthetic signatures generated on the real covariate vectors so that the scarcity of ΔD1 near zero is the real one, 11 of 213 pairs, the absolute bias was at most 0.022 in **32 of 34** cells and coverage of the nominal 95% interval ranged from 0.918 to 0.980 (Figure 5c, d). §6v fixed a single acceptance rule, absolute bias at most 0.05 together with coverage between 0.92 and 0.97 across the whole grid, and the grid meets neither half of it: two cells exceed the bias limit and the coverage range falls outside the band at both ends. I therefore do not report the estimator as well behaved, and give the achieved values instead. The bias half of that verdict is firm. The coverage half is not: with 500 replicates a coverage estimate carries a Monte Carlo standard error near 0.010, the low end of 0.918 is 459 of 500 against a boundary at 460, and requiring the minimum and the maximum of 34 such estimates to fall inside a band 0.05 wide is a demanding rule for a grid of that size. I report the band as unmet because that is what the rule says, not because one replicate should carry a verdict. The exception is instructive and was not predicted: where true retention is near zero and noise is at the high end of the observed range, the estimate is biased **upward**, by 0.054 and 0.061, the only two cells exceeding 0.022, both at a true value of 0.10 with the highest noise level. Because the numerator is an absolute value, noise cannot push it below zero, so a retention fraction near zero is overstated rather than understated. The direction matters for how this paper's numbers should be read: the low values, including DRAIN at 0.310 and the count of signatures below 50%, are conservative. None of the pre-registered failure conditions was met either: no cell falls below 0.90 coverage, and nothing approaches the bias above 0.20 or coverage below 0.80 that would have withdrawn the recommendation. The recommendation in the Discussion therefore stands, qualified by the bias just described and by the unmet acceptance band.

## S8. What each covariate removes, tissue by tissue (§6u)

**What the two covariates remove is tissue-dependent, although they are never interchangeable.** The §6u verdict is distinct in all five cohorts where both covariates were computed, but the picture behind it is not the same. In GSE14520 composition adjustment removes essentially nothing, leaving a median 1.032, while identity adjustment leaves 0.702. In lung the two covariates are all but independent (*R*² = 0.002), and on the locked panel of 36 sets composition adjustment removes **more** than identity adjustment does, leaving 0.699 against 0.845, the reverse of liver. That reversal does not survive the §6ac sensitivity: with the six sets that are not lung sets removed, the two medians are 0.819 for composition against 0.672 for identity, the same order as liver. I therefore report the reversal as an artifact of what the keyword rule admitted and make no claim that the two covariates change places in lung. The medians under each single-covariate model, in every tissue and with the sensitivity beside the locked panel, are in Figure 10c.

What does survive is the disagreement itself: 26 of 36 signatures change side of the 50% threshold depending on which covariate is used, 22 of 30 after the sensitivity, the highest proportion of any cohort in either version. In kidney the two adjustments leave almost the same median, 0.666 under identity against 0.651 under composition, and yet 9 of the 15 signatures change side of the threshold. The two covariates also move by almost the same amount there, K1 falling 0.843 and C1 rising 0.956, so the agreement of the medians is what §6ae would predict from size alone.

What does not follow from size is the per-signature disagreement, and that is the part of the kidney result I rest on. The claim that correcting for non-parenchymal content does nothing is a liver result rather than a general one, since composition adjustment, taken as a single-covariate model, removes an appreciable share of the shift in lung and in kidney under either panel.

## S9. Composition of the lung and kidney comparator panels (§6ac)

Two limits of this exercise are worth stating with the result rather than after it. The kidney panel is small and unlike the others in subject matter: the enumeration returned 18 sets, of which 15 were evaluable, and they are dominated by transplant immunology and kidney aging rather than by renal cancer, whereas 27 of the 36 evaluable lung sets concern lung cancer, four of them as the site of metastasis from another primary. And the keyword rule admitted six sets to the lung panel that are not lung sets at all. Five are the MCCLUNG series, whose first author's surname, McClung, contains the letters LUNG, and which describe cocaine reward, *CREB1* targets and delta-FosB targets in rodent brain; the sixth is an alveolar rhabdomyosarcoma signature, a soft-tissue sarcoma matched on the word alveolar. All six were retained because the rule was fixed before any set was scored and no set was removed after its retention value was seen. A pre-submission audit found them, and §6ac records what removing them does. It leaves the lung distribution and every conclusion drawn from its width unchanged, and it reverses one comparison, which I report with the locked result rather than in place of it.

## S10. Three candidate explanations for the transfer failure, tested (§6aa, §6ab)

**Post-transcriptional divergence does not explain the transfer failure.** The source study measured messenger RNA and protein in the same 159 patients and published a Spearman correlation between them for 6,203 genes, median 0.543 (interquartile range 0.376 to 0.679). Under §6aa I predicted, before reading any of it, that signatures whose members agree poorly between the two levels would be the ones whose retention differs most, at Spearman ρ ≤ −0.30. Across the 97 signatures evaluable at both levels, **ρ = +0.094 (*P* = 0.36)**. The prediction is not supported, and the candidate explanation is withdrawn rather than left standing as a possibility.

The descriptive detail is sharper than the test. The three DRAIN members have among the **highest** mRNA to protein agreement in the whole panel, *MTARC1* at 0.831 (96th percentile), *POR* at 0.785 and *MTARC2* at 0.762, while the REDUCTION members sit near the middle, *CYB5R3* at 0.639 and *CYB5R1* at 0.541. The module whose retention differs most between levels, 25% on transcript against 88% on protein, both from the identity-only model so that the same single covariate is used at each level, is built from the genes whose two measurements agree best. Whatever produces the discordance, it is not that these genes' protein levels fail to follow their transcripts. Figure 12a and 11c show both. Across the 97 signatures, where a composition covariate is available at both levels and the joint model is therefore used on both sides, the median retention is 0.915 at protein level against 0.718 at transcript level, so the shift is systematic and not confined to this study's own panel. The covariate's own movement, four times smaller in this matrix, is the candidate explanation set out above; §6ab's two predictors are not.

**Nor is it an artifact of protein measurement.** A shotgun proteome covers a fraction of the transcriptome and samples it unevenly by abundance [21, 22], so under §6ab I asked, before computing either quantity, whether the discrepancy is larger for signatures whose proteins are scarce in the matrix or whose genes are mostly absent from it. The deposited matrix carries 6,478 proteins quantified in all 318 samples with no missing values, and across the same 97 signatures the proportion of a set's genes that are quantified ranges from 0.15 to 0.91, median 0.59. Neither predictor meets the registered threshold: the discrepancy against mean abundance gives Spearman ρ = **−0.162** (*P* = 0.11) and against proteome coverage ρ = **+0.011** (*P* = 0.92), where the prediction fixed in advance was ρ ≤ −0.30. The two are not equally settled. On 97 signatures the interval around −0.162 runs from −0.350 to +0.039 and still contains −0.30, which makes limited dynamic range unresolved rather than excluded; the interval around +0.011 excludes it. *CYB5R3*, the gene that reverses direction between the two proteomes, sits at the **98th percentile of abundance** in this matrix, and the two mARC members sit mid-range, at the 27th and 41st, with *POR* at the 58th. The modules that disagree between levels are not at the difficult end of the instrument's range.

## S11. The histological-purity covariate and the registered §6aa verdict

Entering the histological purity into the model alongside D1 and C1, where §6aa had specified D1 alone, gives a result that has to be reported in two parts. The rule fixed in §6aa was that either retention fraction moving by more than 0.10 would mean the measure is partly confounded with purity, and by that rule the verdict is that it is, because the REDUCTION fraction moves from 1.85 to 0.61. But REDUCTION has no interpretable ratio in this proteome: its unadjusted shift is −0.069 with *P* = 0.36, and applying the §6v stability guard unchanged gives a bootstrap interval for its denominator of −0.220 to +0.078, which spans zero. A ratio whose denominator is not different from zero moves a long way for arithmetic reasons. On DRAIN, the only module here with a stable denominator (−0.944, interval −1.087 to −0.805), adding histological purity moves retention from 1.109, its value in the joint D1 and C1 model used here, to 1.016, a change of **0.093**, inside the threshold. Purity is not idle in either model, and this is a result rather than a caveat: its own coefficient is significant for both modules, +1.665 (*P* = 0.0079) for REDUCTION and +1.708 (*P* = 0.0011) for DRAIN. What survives its addition is the retention fraction, not the claim that purity is irrelevant.

The rule fired, so the verdict of record is the registered one. The guard I used to set REDUCTION aside comes from §6v and was not part of §6aa, which makes it a post hoc rescue of a registered test that failed, and §6aa required the distinctness claim to be qualified in that event. It is: the distinctness of this measure from purity adjustment rests on the construction argument and on the C1 correlation, not on this test.

## S12. How the identity covariate itself behaves in TCGA-LIHC (§6w)

**The covariate itself behaves differently, in an informative way.** Seventeen of the 22 identity genes are the differentiated effectors, and they agree closely between cohorts, all seventeen falling in both. The five transcription factors do not: their mean paired difference is +0.422 in TCGA-LIHC against −0.269 in GSE14520, and *HNF4A*, *HNF1A* and *FOXA2* reverse sign between the cohorts, rising in the sequencing cohort where they fall on the array (Figure 9d). The identity score is therefore driven by its effector members in both cohorts but by opposing regulator behavior between them. §6u also repeats less emphatically here: the verdict is still distinct, but the two covariates share 26% of their variance rather than 12%, the two retention fractions now correlate positively (ρ = +0.651) rather than negatively, and one of the four pre-registered redundancy criteria is met where none was in GSE14520.

---
